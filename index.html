The ideal connected signal follows the original idea without the prototype's hardware limits. Every car and pedestrian shares position and speed, as in the 2024 GPS + MQTT design. The signal wakes about 7 s before a car reaches the stop line (4 s before a walker reaches the curb), so the approach is already green when they arrive. Green stays on while more traffic keeps arriving from that direction, for up to 20 s if someone is waiting on the cross street. Walkers and drivers are told apart by speed, using the 2.2352 m/s (5 mph) cutoff. The measured prototype setting uses your trial results instead: the Bluetooth connection at 9.87 m, release at 13.35 m, a fixed green, and GPS speed noise of about 0.1 m/s walking and 0.5 m/s driving. Only the direction with someone waiting gets a green, and a waiting pedestrian always gets a full walk. Energy counts lamp power only: eight LED heads at the wattage above. A conventional signal draws that around the clock, so savings track how long the smart signal stays dark. Traffic volumes are illustrative curves with morning and evening peaks, not counts from a real intersection.
